# Original asset guide

The eight basic defender classes use the approved faceted Ranger family: simple low-poly heads, readable solid clothing and equipment, and progressively stronger silhouettes across six ranks. Champions retain their established connected anatomy, tailored clothing and individual designs. Warm timber, pale stone, dull iron, gold heraldry, subdued green/brown orcs and red hide tents unify the roster. Scenery retains stylized faceted ridges. No borrowed game assets or external textures are used. Original fantasy siege machines include a catapult and distinct ballista/cannon variants.

Current release: **0.2.8 · Secret Champions**. Version 0.2.1 introduced the original dwarf carpenter Engineer, replaced Kingslayer's siege machine with a black knight, gave Knight and Lionheart Champion closed plate helmets, equipped Paladin with a warhammer and made Nature Spirit (formerly Mother Nature) a floating spirit. Version 0.2.7 restored Engineer's dwarf identity and recipes; the V3 basic reconstruction keeps that stable identity. The human Kushek design from 0.2.6 remains archived unchanged. Art patch 9 rebuilds Lady Claire independently with a new adult face, natural blonde hairline, gold crown and ivory/champagne gown. The Engineer class and recipes stay restored. Version 0.2.2 gives all 37 champions equally large, strong cosmetic auras: Basic blue, Intermediate green, Advanced purple and TOP gold. These are created at runtime in `game/render/champion-aura.js`, with the verified mapping in `game/render/champion-classification.js`; they do not alter combat buffs. See [all classifications](docs/CHAMPION_CLASSIFICATION.md).

## Scale and origins

- One grid cell = one world meter. A normal tower foundation fits a 0.94 × 0.94 meter square.
- Normal towers reach roughly 1.5–2.2 meters; advanced defenders use distinct hero, creature or siege silhouettes. Scenery keeps may be larger because they are outside the buildable grid.
- Blender uses Z up; GLB export converts to Y up for Three.js. Static material groups originate at the grid center and ground level. Basic reconstruction uses +Y front, +X anatomical right and sole Z=0 before adding the technical foundation; exported models face -Z. Deliberate root transforms, including the dwarf's shorter and broader proportions, remain in the articulated hierarchy.
- Runtime placement offsets grid coordinates by 18 to center the map. Visual scale never changes the strategic footprint.

## Naming and assets

- `human_<family>_t1.glb` through `t6.glb`: 48 character rank variants.
- `advanced_<family>.glb`: 39 fixed champion, creature and siege models (37 champion-v6 plus two champions-v7.10 Secret models).
- `barricade.glb`, `pine_tree.glb`, `deciduous_tree.glb`, `rock.glb`, `castle_wall.glb`, `human_keep.glb`, `medieval_house.glb`, `barrel.glb`, `crate.glb`, `human_banner.glb`, `campfire.glb`, `wooden_fence.glb`, `cliff_module.glb`, `orc_warcamp.glb`.
- `orc_<archetype>.glb`: six original character blockouts, plus simple root/spine armature scaffolds in their source construction.
- `public/assets/models/manifest.json` lists 107 exports: 87 defender variants (48 basic ranks and 39 champions), plus 20 legacy prop/enemy assets. Entries include family/rank identities, style and triangle counts.
- `public/assets/enemies/manifest.json` separately lists 59 active Dark Host models and their portraits (dark-host-v3), with editable source paths, native bounds, wingspan, archetype, semantic parts and cosmetic aura stage.
- `public/assets/designs/kushek/manifest.json` marks the preserved six-rank human Kushek design as inactive. Its GLBs and portraits live in that separate folder; the scene is `blender/scenes/kushek_design_v1.blend`. `author_kushek.py` and the unused `game/render/kushek.js` fallback remain available for a future role.

`game/render/models.js` defines the original defender fallback meshes. `tools/export-defender-meshes.mjs` supplies these to the legacy stage of the full generator. Native Blender authoring scripts then overwrite defender GLBs with the approved archer-style roster; the fallback meshes are retained for immediate rendering while GLBs load. The approved JSON roster is authoritative for champion identities and statistics; the model manifest is authoritative for shipped asset paths. Legacy unreferenced GLBs are not loaded. Representative Royal Archer and Orc Grunt `.blend` scenes are retained under `blender/scenes/`.

## Geometry and materials

Aim for 200–2,500 triangles for normal structures and props. Defender validation enforces a 10,000-triangle ceiling per exported file except Lord Bernhard's mounted composition, which has an explicit 13,500 ceiling. Lady Claire V2 uses 9,550 triangles and Bernhard V2 13,297; his raised sword reaches 2.974 meters, with an explicit 3.1-meter height ceiling. Dark Host authoring budgets target 19,000 triangles for regular designs and 25,000 for the large wave-40/50 bosses, with a hard export ceiling of 30,000; the current maximum is 20,074. The larger scenery landmarks have separate bounded budgets: the current V7 castle town uses 109,570 triangles and 41 mesh batches; the inhabited orc territory uses 139,117 triangles and 25. Static pieces are joined by material to reduce draw calls while preserving useful material groups. The cohesive Blender pass joins and reshapes skin and clothing surfaces and smooths their normals, keeping armor and equipment edges crisp. Articulation parents remain separate where runtime movement requires them. `blender/scripts/cohesive.py` centralizes this treatment and mesh budgeting.

Materials use semantic names such as `MAT_stone`, `MAT_wood`, `MAT_iron`, `MAT_blue` and `MAT_gold`. Magic focus materials have subtle emissive color. PBR base color and roughness are embedded in GLB; there are no runtime image-texture dependencies. Final art can add UV-mapped atlases while retaining these slots.

V3 basic defenders retain broad flat facets and real cloth thickness. Their silhouette comes from the measured anatomy, garment shells and equipment meshes; the champion smoothing pass must not replace that construction.

## Export and regeneration

```sh
node tools/export-defender-meshes.mjs
blender --background --python blender/scripts/generate_assets.py -- --output public/assets/models
```

The script was executed and verified with Blender 5.2.2; it is written against Blender 4.x/5.x APIs. Export is binary glTF 2.0, Y up, transforms applied, no cameras/lights and no final animation clips. Blender must be installed only to regenerate models, not to play.

The browser loads all 87 playable defender GLBs and the separate 59-model enemy pack, with procedural fallbacks while loading. Basic reconstruction uses the `hooded-turnarounds-v3` style; the other packs use champion-v6, champions-v7.10, dark-host-v3, scenery-v7 (castle) and scenery-v6 (camp). The public manifest identifies which revision is included in a build. All 87 defender portraits are rendered directly by Blender and shared by draft cards, the grimoire and the Royal atelier through `assets/army/<family>-t<rank>.png`, including Archer. The art release query invalidates older cached models, manifests and portraits without changing the game's leaderboard edition. The approved catalog contains 39 champions with individual character, creature or siege identities. Stable family IDs preserve existing recipes and discoveries. Ground scenery remains procedural Three.js geometry in `game/render/environment.js`, merged by material where static; legacy prop GLBs are retained as references.

## Animation and VFX

Browser enemy limbs use exported pivots for walk cycles, bobbing, heading and hit reactions. `game/render/enemy-motion.js` rotates each native wing around its Y-up-compatible hinge: bat cycles are fast and broad, armored bats slower, and manta/wyvern cycles slower still. Ground legs, body breathing and sway give living actors motion; the camp preview uses the same helper with walking disabled. Actual combat animation uses a paused simulation clock. Reduced motion restores authored wing, leg and body poses, while functional hit feedback remains. On death, the same model falls into a ground pose based on its actual geometry; flying enemies descend and fold their wings. All 59 corpse forms rest on terrain without altering cached geometry or simultaneously living clones. Corpses remain through combat and clear when construction begins. Dark Host's five cosmetic aura stages follow waves 1–10 none, 11–20 yellow, 21–30 red, 31–40 dark violet and 41–50 black with a violet edge. Ground effects surround feet and the lower body; flight effects follow the body and wing roots, with stronger boss effects. These stages do not grant buffs or alter the distinct champion classification auras. Defenders breathe, sway and aim; all 87 exports carry real articulated limb, weapon, orb or horse geometry. Archer's drawing hand pulls a runtime string between exported bow tips and the nock. Soldier's shoulder, elbow and wrist carry its blade through a swing. Casters raise their staffs and glow at the focus; spell waves and projectiles use the moving world-space weapon tip. Dragon jaws/wings and siege mechanisms retain their dedicated motions. These remain rigid articulated miniatures, rather than skinned characters with authored animation clips. Cosmetic geometry, motion and sounds are bounded and cleaned up without changing combat statistics.

`blender/scripts/articulation.py` authors the rigid joint hierarchy. Export batching preserves each joint and its child meshes; `battle-animation.js` moves only cloned transforms. Shared source positions, normals and materials remain unchanged. Each instance owns its dynamic string/glow resources and disposes them when rebuilt or removed. Articulation costs more draw calls: the current average is 32 material/joint batches per defender (maximum 47), compared with roughly 12 before articulation; the 85 established defenders and Lady Claire remain under 10,000 triangles, with the explicit mounted-Bernhard exception described above. Idle rigs return without updating their joint geometry.

The river uses curve-aligned procedural shading and traveling foam streaks. The waterfall has flowing sheets, descending spray and expanding splash rings. Detailed branched trees and terraced peaks remain outside every build tile. Soft procedural cloud sprites drift and fade in with camera distance; close views hide them.

Held caster tools use the right weapon joint and a moving `staff_tip`; books remain under the left weapon joint. Geometry and materials stay shared while every placed unit receives private transforms.

Control renders are written to `blender/renders/hooded-turnarounds-v3/<family>-t<rank>-{front,back,left,right,three-quarter}.png`. Four orthographic views per rank provide 192 reference comparisons; three-quarter views check actual volume. Separate `-effects.png` presentations show native preview VFX. Only the camera changes between geometry views. Family metrics record native/export triangle counts, bounds, semantic parts, landmarks, checksums and GLB reimport checks; authoring measurements and inventories are in `blender/scripts/defender_turnarounds_v3/*-measurements.json`. The technical checks are not a claim of pixel-perfect visual reconstruction. Consult the measured comparison report and disclosed ambiguities for the achieved silhouette/landmark deviations; the requested IoU 0.97 and 1% landmark targets are goals, not guaranteed results.
